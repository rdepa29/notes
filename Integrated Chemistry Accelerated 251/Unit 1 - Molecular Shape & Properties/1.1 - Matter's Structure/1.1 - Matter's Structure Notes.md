# Atomic Structure
## The Basics
### Parts of an Atom
*An atom contains two major parts: the inner nucleus, and the outer electron cloud.*
#### Nucleus
- Composed of protons & neutrons
- Contains basically the entire mass of an atom
- Positively charged
#### Electron Cloud
- Electrons "orbit" the nucleus
- Split into Shells, Subshells, and Orbitals
	- Shells
		- Most general way to describe the electron cloud
		- Split by quantum number
			- 2, 3, ..., 6, 7, 8
				- 8 can only be accomplished through synthetic materials, and can only be incomplete for now
			- Highest shell determines valence electrons
	- Subshells
		- Describes the amount of orbitals in a shell
		- 2s, 2p, 3s, ...
	- Orbitals
		- Most specific way to describe the electron cloud
		- Each orbital holds two electrons
		- Orbitals are split by shapes
			- s, p, f, d
- Electrons don't occupy a single point (or velocity/energy), they have a probability to be anywhere in their orbital
### Protons, Neutrons, & Electrons
*Atoms are composed of three primary subatomic particles: The proton, the neutron, and the electron.*
#### The Proton ($p^+$)
- Positive charge
#### The Neutron ($n^0$)
- Neutral charge
#### The Electron ($e^-$)
- Negative charge

### Electron Configuration
*A way to describe the Shells, Subshells, and Orbitals an atom occupies*
- List out using: {Shell}{Orbital}$^{e^-\ count}$
- Example for Oxygen (O): $1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^4$
	- Shell 1
		- Shell 1 only contains the $s$ orbital, so we only get $1s^2$ before moving to the next shell
	- Shell 2
		- Shell 2 has two orbitals; the $s$ orbital and the $p$ orbital
			- the $s$ orbital only holds 2 electrons, as there is only one possible shape
			- the $p$ orbital has 3 possible shapes ($p_x,\ p_y,\ \&\  p_z$), so it can hold 6 electrons when full
		- Shell 2 is also Oxygen's valence shell, as it is the highest energy level electrons can reach
